Generative AI

A Training Session for Educators

What is Generative AI?

Generative AI is a field of Artificial Intelligence that focuses on creating new, original content rather than just analyzing or classifying existing data. It learns patterns and structures from large datasets and then uses that knowledge to produce novel outputs.

Think of it as a student who has studied a vast library of books and can now write their own original stories, inspired by what they've read but entirely new.

  • Generates images, videos, text, music, and more.
  • Moves beyond prediction to creation.
  • Combines technology with human-like imagination.

Deep Dive: GenAI in Real Life

Marketing: Companies use tools like Jasper or Copy.ai to instantly generate ad copy, social media posts, and product descriptions, dramatically speeding up content creation.

Entertainment: Disney used proprietary Generative AI tools to create age-reversal effects for actors in films like 'The Irishman', generating realistic younger versions of the actors' faces.

Traditional AI vs. Generative AI

While both are subsets of AI, their goals are fundamentally different. Traditional AI is like a skilled chef following a recipe perfectly every time. Generative AI is like an experimental chef inventing entirely new dishes.

Feature Traditional AI (Discriminative) Generative AI
Focus Defined tasks, problem-solving, classification Content creation, exploration
Output Predictions, decisions (e.g., "Is this a cat?") New content (e.g., "Generate a picture of a cat.")
Example Facial recognition, spam filters ChatGPT, Midjourney, DALL-E

Key Drivers of the GenAI Boom

The recent explosion in Generative AI is not accidental. It's powered by two key technological advancements happening simultaneously.

1. Massive Data Availability

GenAI models are "trained" on enormous datasets. The internet has provided a vast library of text, images, and code for these models to learn from, like the Common Crawl dataset which contains petabytes of web data.

2. Computational Power & Algorithms

Advanced algorithms like Deep Learning and Transformers can process this data. This is made possible by powerful hardware, especially Graphics Processing Units (GPUs), which can perform the millions of calculations needed.

Deep Dive: GenAI in Real Life

Hardware Impact: NVIDIA, a company that originally made GPUs for gaming, is now a dominant force in AI. Their H100 and A100 GPUs are the workhorses in data centers that train large models like ChatGPT. The demand is so high it has created a global supply shortage.

Open Data: Initiatives like Wikipedia and open-source code on GitHub have provided high-quality, structured data that is crucial for training reliable AI models on a massive scale.

Types of GenAI: GANs

Generative Adversarial Networks

GANs use a clever "cat and mouse" system with two competing neural networks:

  • The Generator: An "artist" that creates new content (e.g., an image). Its goal is to create content so realistic it fools the Discriminator.
  • The Discriminator: An "art critic" that compares the Generator's creation to real examples. Its goal is to correctly identify whether the content is real or fake.

They train together, constantly competing. This process forces the Generator to become incredibly skilled at creating lifelike, high-quality outputs.

Deep Dive: GANs in Real Life

Fashion & E-commerce: The website "This Person Does Not Exist" uses a GAN (specifically, NVIDIA's StyleGAN) to create an endless stream of realistic human faces. Retailers use this tech to generate synthetic models to display clothing, saving on photoshoot costs.

Video Games: Game developers use GANs to automatically generate realistic textures for environments, such as stone, wood, or terrain, significantly speeding up the game design process.

Types of GenAI: RNNs

Recurrent Neural Networks

RNNs are designed to work with sequential data—data where the order matters, like text, music, or speech.

Their key feature is a form of "memory." As an RNN processes a sequence, it remembers information from previous steps. This context is crucial for predicting what should come next.

Think of it as reading a sentence: to understand the last word, you need to remember the words that came before it. RNNs do the same, making them excellent for tasks like language translation and text generation.

Deep Dive: RNNs in Real Life

Predictive Text: The auto-complete feature on your smartphone keyboard or Google Search uses RNN-like models. It analyzes the sequence of letters you've typed to predict the most likely next word.

Coding Assistants: Tools like GitHub Copilot are built on an evolution of RNNs (Transformers). They analyze the sequence of code you've written to suggest entire lines or functions, understanding the context of your program.

Types of GenAI: VAEs

Variational Autoencoders

VAEs are excellent at learning the essential characteristics of data in a compressed, efficient way. They consist of two parts:

  • The Encoder: This part analyzes input data (like a face) and compresses it into a simplified numerical representation, capturing key features like smile, hair color, etc.
  • The Decoder: This part takes the compressed representation and reconstructs the original data.

By tweaking the numbers in the compressed form before decoding, VAEs can generate new, similar data. For example, you could increase the "smile" value to generate a happier-looking face.

Deep Dive: VAEs in Real Life

Drug Discovery: Scientists use VAEs to generate new molecular structures. An encoder learns the essential properties of known effective drug molecules, and the decoder then generates novel variations that can be tested, potentially leading to new medicines faster.

Creative Tools: In photo editing software, VAEs power features that allow for smooth transformations, like morphing one face into another or altering facial expressions realistically by manipulating the underlying compressed features.

The Tech Behind Text-to-Image

How does a tool like Midjourney or DALL-E turn a sentence into a picture? It's a combination of different AI technologies working together.

1. Natural Language Processing (NLP)

This is the "translator." It takes your text prompt (e.g., "A cat wearing a hat") and breaks it down, understanding the objects ("cat," "hat") and their relationship ("wearing").

2. Machine Learning (ML) & Computer Vision (CV)

The model is trained on billions of image-caption pairs. Through this training, it learns the connection between words and visuals. CV helps it understand visual details—the shape of a cat, the texture of a hat.

3. The Generative Model

Finally, a generative model (often a "diffusion model") uses the understanding from NLP and ML to create a brand-new image pixel by pixel that matches your prompt.

Deep Dive: Real-Life Synergy

Integrated Creative Suites: Adobe Firefly is now embedded in Photoshop. A user can select an area of an image and type a prompt like "add a flock of birds," and the AI generates them directly into the existing picture, seamlessly blending them.

Accessible Design: Microsoft Designer (formerly Bing Image Creator) integrates DALL-E 3 directly into its design platform. This allows users without artistic skill to generate high-quality custom graphics for presentations, posters, or social media just by describing them.

GenAI in the Classroom

Generative AI offers powerful tools for both teachers and students, transforming how we approach lesson planning, content creation, and personalized learning.

For Teachers:

  • Lesson Planning: Generate lesson plans, activity ideas, and quiz questions tailored to a specific topic and grade level.
  • Differentiated Instruction: Create multiple versions of a text at different reading levels with a single click.
  • Material Creation: Instantly generate example problems, stories, or images to illustrate a concept.

For Students:

  • Personalized Tutors: AI can act as a practice partner, explaining concepts in different ways and providing instant feedback on problems.
  • Creative Spark: Overcome writer's block by generating story ideas or visualize a concept by creating an image from a description.
  • Research Assistant: Summarize long articles or explain complex topics in simpler terms.

Deep Dive: Real-Life EdTech Tools

MagicSchool.ai: A platform specifically for educators that offers dozens of tools, including a rubric generator, a lesson plan creator, and a tool to generate Individualized Education Program (IEP) goals, saving teachers hours of administrative work.

Khanmigo: Khan Academy's AI-powered guide acts as both a student tutor and a teacher assistant. It can guide a student through a math problem without giving the answer, and it can help teachers write class outlines.

Curipod: This tool allows teachers to build interactive presentations with AI-generated polls, word clouds, and drawing activities based on a simple lesson topic prompt.

Ethical Considerations

With great power comes great responsibility. The rise of Generative AI introduces significant ethical challenges that we must navigate carefully.

Bias and Discrimination

Models are trained on real-world data, which contains human biases. If training data underrepresents certain groups, the AI can reproduce or amplify stereotypes.

Misinformation & Deepfakes

GenAI can create highly realistic but entirely fake images, articles, and videos ("deepfakes"). This can be used to spread false information, manipulate public opinion, or create malicious content.

Intellectual Property & Plagiarism

Who owns AI-generated content? The user who wrote the prompt, the company that built the AI, or the creators of the original data it was trained on? This is a major legal gray area.

Deep Dive: Real-Life Ethical Dilemmas

Copyright Lawsuits: The New York Times is suing OpenAI and Microsoft, alleging that ChatGPT was trained on millions of their copyrighted articles without permission, and that it can now generate text that directly competes with their journalism.

Political Deepfakes: During election cycles, AI-generated audio and video clips have emerged that falsely depict candidates saying things they never said, creating confusion and aiming to mislead voters.

Bias in Image Models: Early image generators, when prompted with "CEO" or "doctor," predominantly created images of white men, reflecting and reinforcing societal biases present in their training data. Companies are now actively working to mitigate this.

Guidelines for Responsible Student Use

It's crucial to teach students to use Generative AI as a powerful tool, not a crutch. The goal is to enhance learning, not replace it.

Responsible Use (Supplement) Irresponsible Use (Substitute)
Using AI to brainstorm ideas for an essay. Asking AI to write the entire essay.
Asking AI to summarize a complex scientific paper. Copying the summary without reading the original paper.
Using AI to generate practice questions to test your knowledge. Using AI to find the answers to homework questions.
Using AI to create an illustration for a story you wrote. Claiming the AI's illustration as your own original artwork.

The Core Principle: Use AI to support the processes of critical thinking, research, and creativity, but never to bypass them. The student must remain the author and thinker.

Key Takeaways

GenAI Creates: Its primary function is to generate new, original content.

It's a Tool, Not Magic: It's powered by massive data, powerful hardware, and complex algorithms.

Huge Potential in Education: It can revolutionize lesson planning and personalized learning.

Ethics are Paramount: We must teach and practice responsible use, being mindful of bias, misinformation, and academic integrity.
